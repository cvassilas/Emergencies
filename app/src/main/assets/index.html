Displaced endotracheal tube (ETT)
  • Shout for help. Disconnect the ventilator from the tube and ventilate with a bag-valve-mask on 100% O2.
  • Check: chest rise, EtCO2 trace, SpO2, bilateral air entry, and gastric sounds.
  • Work through DOPE: Displacement (tube out or too deep/mainstem), Obstruction (secretions, plug, kink), Pneumothorax (tension), Equipment (circuit, cuff, leak).
  • If the tube is out or in doubt, re-intubate with a bougie. Confirm with EtCO2 and auscultation, then CXR.
  • If unable to intubate or oxygenate: follow CICO and perform a scalpel-bougie-tube cricothyroidotomy.
Displaced tracheostomy
  • Call for help immediately (anaesthetist and ENT / tracheostomy team). Give 100% O2 by bag-valve-mask over the mouth and nose and the stoma together.
  • Early (tract not mature, usually first 7–10 days): do not blindly reinsert through a fresh stoma. Oral intubation is the preferred route. Use a bougie and laryngoscope, and keep the ETT size in mind.
  • If oral intubation fails, reinsert a tracheostomy tube through the stoma with a bougie/dilator under direct vision.
  • Mature tract: reinsert the tracheostomy using the same size or one size smaller, with a bougie. Check EtCO2 and chest rise.
  • If it is not possible to oxygenate or intubate, use CICO: scalpel-bougie-tube cricothyroidotomy or the emergency cricothyroidotomy kit.
  • Keep the old tube and the spare tube at the bedside. Document the event on the adverse-event log.
Tension pneumothorax
  • Clinical diagnosis: absent breath sounds on one side, tracheal deviation, hypotension, raised airway pressure, distended neck veins.
  • Decompress immediately (large-bore cannula 2nd intercostal space mid-clavicular line, or 4th–5th intercostal space anterior axillary line), then insert a chest drain.
Pulmonary oedema / shock / bradycardia
  • Bradycardia with poor perfusion: atropine (adult 500 mcg; child 20 mcg/kg, min 100 mcg) and/or adrenaline infusion, then pacing if needed.
  • Vasoactive support: noradrenaline first-line for shock. Use central access (see Infusions).
Anaphylaxis
  • IM adrenaline 500 mcg (adult, 1:1000) into the anterolateral thigh. Repeat every 5 min if needed.
  • IV hydrocortisone and chlorphenamine as adjuncts. IV fluids for hypotension.

Procedure safety checklists (LocSSIP)

Use the full printed LocSSIP form for documentation. Key points below for quick reference.
Intubation (ITU)
  • Team roles (intubator, drugs, assistant, cricoid, MILS). Difficult airway trolley ready.
  • Pre-oxygenate 100% for 3 min. Nasal cannulae for apnoeic oxygenation if needed.
  • Spinal precautions where indicated. Water's circuit ready.
  • Post intubation: confirm position (EtCO2 trace, bilateral air entry), tube depth, cuff pressure, ventilator settings, sedation, CXR, handover.
Tracheostomy
  • Confirm the team, roles and equipment, including bronchoscope, spare tubes and difficult airway plan.
  • Check coagulation and consent. Hold feeds and aspirate NG.
  • Post procedure: position of the tube (bronchoscopy if used), cuff pressure, EtCO2, CXR as required, bedhead sign.
Bronchoscopy
  • Confirm ventilator settings (100% FiO2, PEEP appropriate), sedation and paralysis, and that capnography is in situ.
  • Check coagulation and contraindications (high FiO2 or PEEP, anatomical, vascular, coagulopathy).
  • Record BAL and tissue samples sent and the sedation given.
Central venous catheter (CVC)
  • Consent, coagulation, drug allergies. Two-person team (operator and assistant).
  • 2% chlorhexidine / 70% isopropyl alcohol skin prep. Large sterile drape. Full sterile barrier.
  • Ultrasound-guided. Check every lumen aspirates and flushes. Confirm placement (guidewire removed, CVP waveform, US, CXR as indicated).
  • Paired venous and arterial gases if position or concern about the line. Record CVP.
Chest drain
  • Safe triangle (anterior to mid-axillary line, above nipple, below axilla). Ultrasound guidance if available.
  • Stop if unable to aspirate air or fluid during LA infiltration.
  • Effusion: no more than 500 mL in the first hour and no more than 1500 mL in the first 24 h.
  • Check sutures and dressing, drain not elevated above the chest, and CXR for position.

Renal Drug Handbook (CRRT / CVVHD)

Excerpt from The Renal Drug Handbook, 5th ed. (UK Renal Pharmacy Group, CRC Press). The full 5th edition text is searched below. The curated CVVHD summaries above are from the earlier preview (A–Aspirin). The handbook says CAV/VVH dosing is the same as CAV/VVHD unless stated, and that CRRT dosing should be reviewed against the actual flux and GFR.
CRRT flux method (from the Handbook, ampicillin monograph): add blood flow, citrate, dialysate and replacement flow (mL/hr), divide by 60, and use the result as the patient's GFR in mL/min. Example: 100 + 1000 + 1000 + 200 mL/hr total 2200 mL/hr → GFR ≈ 36.7 mL/min. Then dose by GFR, not the dialysis recommendation.
